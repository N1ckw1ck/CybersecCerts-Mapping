Provider Notes

Notes and musings on certain certification providers, based on personal experience/research. Not exhaustive.

Mile2

Fairly well regarded in the InfoSec community, but better known in government or military circles than in private sector.

mile2.com/role-based-certification-roadmap

ISACA

Exam prices drop with membership, which carries an annual fee - this can be worthwhile especially for students and recent graduates. ISACA certifications require provable years of work experience; education can fulfill some but not all of this requirement.

isaca.org/credentialing

EC-Council

Several certifications require either provable work experience or completion of official paid training to qualify for the exam. Without work experience, the required training can be very expensive - often over $1,600 for a single course tied to one cert.

EC-Council also practices dynamic pricing based on visitor data, meaning different people may see different prices for the same product. Checking the site in an incognito browser, on a mobile device, or from a different IP address may show a better price.

eccouncil.org/train-certify

GIAC

Certifications are well valued but expensive. Most exams alone cost around $1,000, and the associated training can range from a few thousand to ten thousand dollars. Self-funding this training is generally not recommended — most people obtain it through an employer or school.

giac.org/certifications

About This Roadmap

The Security Certification Roadmap is an attempt to map out a good number of useful cybersecurity related/adjacent certifications, taxonomized by sectors within the industry and difficulty to attain. The II denotes that this is a second take on the original, rewritten in Javascript and significantly updated to reflect changes over the past 2+ years.
